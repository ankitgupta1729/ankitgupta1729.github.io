Compound interest is often called the eighth wonder of the world. The phrase is a cliché — but the math behind it is genuinely staggering, and most people underestimate it because human intuition is stubbornly linear while compounding is exponential.

## Linear vs. exponential intuition

Ask someone to fold a piece of paper 42 times and guess its thickness. Most say "a few centimeters." The real answer: thick enough to reach the Moon. Each fold *doubles* the previous one. Our brains simply aren't wired for repeated multiplication — and that blind spot is exactly why compounding feels like magic.

## The formula, decoded

```
Future Value = P × (1 + r)ⁿ
```

- **P** — your principal (starting amount)
- **r** — return per period
- **n** — number of periods

The power lives in that exponent `n`. Returns earn returns, which themselves earn returns. The effect is small at first and then, suddenly, enormous.

## Why starting early beats investing more

Consider two savers, both earning 8% annually:

- **Anita** invests $5,000/year from age 25 to 35 (10 years, $50,000 total), then stops and never adds another dollar.
- **Ben** waits, then invests $5,000/year from age 35 to 65 (30 years, $150,000 total).

At 65, despite investing **three times more money**, Ben ends up with *less* than Anita. Her early contributions had decades longer to compound. Time in the market is the dominant variable — not the amount, and certainly not timing the market.

## The "Rule of 72"

A handy mental shortcut: divide 72 by your annual return to estimate how long your money takes to double.

- At 8%: 72 / 8 = **9 years** to double
- At 4%: 72 / 4 = **18 years**
- At 12%: 72 / 12 = **6 years**

It's an approximation, but it's remarkably good and lets you reason about growth without a calculator.

## Compounding cuts both ways

The same force works *against* you:

- **Debt.** Credit card interest at 24% APR doubles what you owe in roughly three years if unpaid. Compounding is brutal when you're on the wrong side of it.
- **Fees.** A 2% annual fee sounds small. Over 30 years it can quietly consume a third or more of your final balance — because that 2% would have compounded too.
- **Inflation.** At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years. Money sitting idle silently loses purchasing power.

## The honest lesson

You don't need to be brilliant to build wealth. You need to start early, keep costs low, avoid high-interest debt, and let time do the heavy lifting. The hardest part isn't the math — it's the patience. Compounding rewards those who can leave it alone.
